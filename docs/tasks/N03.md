## Goal
A cashier (or anyone with `collection.create`) at **any branch** finds a customer or a contract, sees what is due and overdue, and takes a payment on **one or several of the customer's contracts** in **one request**, split across cash and bank transfers if the customer wants. In one database transaction the server locks the contracts, allocates the amount to the installments oldest first (profit, then principal), writes the payment rows with their allocations under **one receipt**, puts the cash into the drawer of the shift that took it, closes each contract when nothing is left on it, and writes the audit row. The customer can never pay more than what is still owed. No sale, no revenue, no VAT, **no late fee** (Islamic finance rule 4). A retried request returns the same receipt. A wrong collection taken today can be **reversed** the same day (the money goes back to the customer and the installments are unpaid again).

## Slice text (N03, as changed by the owner on 16 Oct)
- **Includes:** find a customer or contract; see what's due and overdue; take a payment (full, partial or several installments, on one or several contracts of the customer), split across methods; allocated in a fixed order: oldest installment (profit, then principal) → next; the customer can't pay more than what is owed; payment receipt (numbered, printed); cash goes to the drawer of the shift that took it, at any branch (collector wallet, N06); contract balance and status update; fully paid → completed; a wrong collection can be reversed on the same day; a collection is **not** a new sale (no new revenue or VAT on the goods); idempotent.
- **Done when:** allocation is the same every time for the same inputs; a double-click records one payment; the last payment closes the contract; a same-day reversal leaves the contract exactly as it was before the collection.

## Owner decisions (16 Oct, answers to the first draft's questions)
1. **Any branch:** a payment can be taken at any branch of the shop. The money goes into the drawer of the branch (shift) that took it; the amount is taken off the customer's installments on the contract, whatever branch the contract was made at.
2. **Split methods:** one collection may be paid with several methods (cash + bank transfers).
3. **No late fees:** the system does not support late fees or late penalties of any kind (against Islamic finance). N03 ignores `charges` completely; N04 was rewritten to overdue tracking only (EC-83).
4. **No overpayment, no store credit:** a customer can't pay more than what is still owed on the contract. Store credit is not a collection method (the system doesn't use customer store credit for collections now).
5. **Reversal allowed:** a wrong collection can be undone. Example: on the 12th a customer pays the installments of the 12th and the 13th, then changes their mind, takes the money back, and pays the 13th on the 13th.
6. **Several contracts, one payment** (second round, 16 Oct): one collection may pay several contracts of the same customer.
7. **Who may reverse** (second round): owner and branch manager by default; the key can be given to any employee when the employee is created or edited.
8. **Same day only** (second round): a reversal is only possible on the collection's business day; older mistakes go through N05.
9. **All contracts visible** (second round): a person holding the collection keys (`collection.create` or `collection.reverse`) sees every contract of the shop, whatever the branch.

## Decisions (record them in D78; every question of the drafts was answered by the owner on 16 Oct)
1. **Who and where:** `collection.create` (`app.require_permission`, so an expired, read-only shop is refused), module `installments`. A counter collection belongs to the caller's open shift (`app.require_open_shift`, locked `FOR UPDATE` like `post_sale`), its still-paired device (`app.require_shift_device(shift_id)`, EC-147), `app.require_branch_access(shift branch)`. The payment rows, the cash movement and the receipt belong to the **shift's** branch; the contract may belong to **any branch of the shop** (owner decision 1). The shop's `installments_enabled` switch is **not** checked: switching installments off stops new contracts, never the collection of old ones.
2. **One or several contracts of one customer, several methods (owner decisions 2 and 6).** `public.post_collection(p_body jsonb, p_idempotency_key text) RETURNS jsonb`. Body (strict): `{ customer_id: Uuid, contracts: [{ contract_id: Uuid, amount: Money (> 0), expected_remaining?: Money }] (1-10, unique contract ids), payments: [1-4 lines] }`. The cashier says how much goes to each contract (the screen proposes it, decision 12); the server never moves money between contracts on its own. Every contract must be this customer's (`CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` otherwise, also for another shop's). A payment line is S02's shape without store credit: `{ method: 'cash', amount, tendered? }` or `{ method: 'bank_transfer', amount, cash_account_id, reference }`; at most one cash line and three bank lines; every amount > 0 with at most 2 decimals; `tendered` ≥ the cash amount (change = tendered − amount, normal change, not an overpayment). Reuse `app.sale_payments_input` for the shape, then refuse `store_credit` (`VALIDATION_FAILED: payment n method`). The payment lines must add up to the sum of the contract amounts (`PAYMENT_TOTAL_MISMATCH {total, paid}`, S02).
3. **No overpayment:** a contract's amount > its current `remaining` → new `COLLECTION_EXCEEDS_REMAINING` (409, `{contract_id, remaining}`). Nothing goes to store credit; N03 writes no `store_credit_movements` (owner decision 4).
4. **Allocation order (fixed, deterministic, in SQL only):** `app.collection_allocation(p_org, p_contract_id, p_amount)` (pure read, `STABLE`, no grants; used by the preview and by the write, so both always agree): the current schedule (`superseded = false`, `version = contracts.current_version`), lines ordered by `seq` (= oldest due date first) with `remaining > 0`. Inside a line: **profit first** (`profit − paid_profit`), **then principal** (`principal − paid_principal`); the next line only when this one is fully paid. Numbers come from `v_schedule_line_status` (effective allocations only: a reversed payment never counts). `charges` are **never** read or paid (owner decision 3). Same contract state + same amount → the same rows in the same order, always (tested by running it twice and comparing). Splitting profit and principal is accounting only (rule 9).
5. **Spreading the allocation over the payment lines:** walk the allocation (contract by contract in the body's order, inside each contract line by line, profit then principal) and the payment lines (in the body's order) together and cut where either runs out, so each payment row gets its own `payment_allocations` rows and every row of one schedule line still has `principal_amount + profit_amount = amount`. Sum of a payment row's allocations = its amount; sum over the collection = the collection amount. Deterministic for the same body.
6. **One receipt for several payment rows (schema change):** add `payments.collection_id uuid` (nullable; composite FK `(collection_id, organization_id)` → `payments (id, organization_id)`; index `(organization_id, collection_id)`). Every row of one collection carries the **first** row's id (the first row points at itself: take its id with `gen_random_uuid()` before the insert). The **receipt number** is the first row's `number`; the other rows keep their own payment numbers (shown in small print). The first row's `idempotency_key` = the request's key; rows 2..n get `system:collection:<first id>:<n>` (S02's pattern). Sale payments leave `collection_id` NULL. Update `docs/EcoTech_Pro_Data_Model.md` (`payments` row).
7. **Order inside `post_collection`** (one transaction): body shape → permission, module → idempotency (`pg_advisory_xact_lock` on the key, then look it up: the same key returns the first answer and changes nothing; a key already used by another payment, e.g. a sale's → `IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT`) → shift lock + device + branch access → customer (the shop's, `CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`) → **contract locks** in contract-id order, so two tills never deadlock (`SELECT … FROM contracts WHERE id = ANY($1) AND organization_id = org AND customer_id = $2 ORDER BY id FOR UPDATE`; a missing one → `CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND {contract_id}`) → then per contract in the body's order: status `active` or `rescheduled` with `remaining > 0`, else new `CONTRACT_NOT_OPEN` (409, `{contract_id, status}`) → `expected_remaining` given and ≠ the server's `remaining` → new `COLLECTION_DUE_CHANGED` (409, `{contract_id, remaining}`: another till took or reversed a payment since the screen loaded) → `COLLECTION_EXCEEDS_REMAINING` → `PAYMENT_TOTAL_MISMATCH` → bank accounts (the shop's, `kind = 'bank'`, active, `FOR SHARE`; S02's `app.sale_payments_check` if it fits, say so) → allocation (decisions 4-5) → payment numbers → `payments` rows → allocations → cash / bank movements → completion (decision 9) → audit → answer. Numbers taken after every check, so a refused collection takes none.
8. **The payment rows and the money:** each row `direction 'in'`, its `method` and `amount`, `customer_id` = the contract's customer, `branch_id` = the shift's branch, `cash_account_id` = the shift's drawer (cash) / the bank account (bank transfer), `shift_id`, `reference`, `tendered` (cash), `received_by` / `created_by` = the employee, `business_date` = `app.org_today(org)`, `collection_id` (decision 6). Cash → one `cash_movements` row on the shift's drawer `+amount`, `type 'collection'`, `source_type 'payment'`, `source_id` = the cash row, note = receipt number + the contract numbers. Bank → one row on the bank account, no shift, as S02 does. The day-close trigger (`DAY_CLOSED`) applies. Collector wallet (N06) is **not built**: write the core as `app.post_collection_core(p_org, p_emp, p_branch, p_shift, p_cash_account, p_body, p_key)` so N06 can call it with a wallet account and no shift.
9. **Completion (per contract):** after the allocations, every contract of the collection whose current-version `remaining` = 0 → `UPDATE contracts SET status = 'completed', closed_at = now(), updated_by = emp` (the baseline `trg_contracts_frozen` lets status / closed_at move; touch nothing else). R03's exposure drops for it; every line shows `paid`. A completed contract takes no more collections (`CONTRACT_NOT_OPEN`).
10. **Not a sale:** no `sales` row, no tax, no ETA receipt (T02), no stock, no revenue. The profit part of each allocation is what reports (D0x) later read as installment profit received.
11. **Reversal (owner decision 5):** `POST /api/collections/:id/reverse` → `public.reverse_collection(p_payment_id uuid, p_body jsonb, p_idempotency_key text)`, body `{ reason: string 3-500 }`, `:id` = the receipt (first row) id.
    - **Who (owner decision 7):** new permission `collection.reverse` (module `installments`), in `role_permissions` for **OWNER and BRANCH_MANAGER** only. Add the key to the shared permission list (the `permissions` table **and** the contracts' permission keys list that F03/F06 keep in sync) so the owner can give it to any employee when creating or editing them (F06's grant rules apply unchanged: a non-owner with `employee.manage` can grant it only if they hold it). `app.require_permission`, so an expired shop is refused.
    - **When (owner decision 8):** only on the **same business day** as the collection (shop timezone), and only when it is the **latest effective collection of every contract it paid** (a later collection on any of them must be reversed first, so the oldest-first order always holds). Otherwise new `COLLECTION_NOT_REVERSIBLE` (409, `{reason: 'not_same_day' | 'not_latest' | 'already_reversed', contract_id?}`). Older mistakes are corrected through N05. A reversal always undoes the whole collection (every contract, every payment line).
    - **Where the money comes from:** the caller's open shift (`require_open_shift`, `require_shift_device`) at the **collection's branch** (`COLLECTION_NOT_REVERSIBLE {reason: 'other_branch'}` otherwise). Cash parts are paid out of that shift's drawer (`cash_movements` `−amount`, type `collection`, `reverses_movement_id` = the original movement; the drawer can't go below 0, the baseline trigger says so); bank parts are recorded as returned from the same bank account (`−amount`, no shift).

No allocations (`v_effective_allocations` already drops both the original and the reversing row, so the installments are unpaid again).
    - **Contracts:** lock them first (contract-id order). Each one that is `completed` (by this collection, the only one that can be latest) → back to `rescheduled` when `current_version > 1`, else `active`, `closed_at = NULL`. After the reversal every contract's lines, balance, R03 exposure and R02 account equal what they were before the collection (tested).
    - **Idempotency:** the same key returns the first answer; a second reversal with another key → `already_reversed`.
    - **Audit:** `collection.reversed` (severity `warning`, entity = the original receipt, `after` = reversal number, amount, methods, reason, per contract: number, status before/after, remaining after) + `contract.reopened` as an extra action when it reopened a contract.
