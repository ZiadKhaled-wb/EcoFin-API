## Goal
The owner (or the accountant) picks a period and sees **what sold and how much profit it made**: grouped by day, month, branch, employee, category, product, payment type or cash vs installment; for each group the quantity sold, the revenue, the cost and the margin; **returns in their own columns** next to the sales, and the net after returns. The grand totals equal the sales documents of the period to the piaster (the slice's Done when). The numbers come from SQL; the browser never adds money up.

## Decisions (record them in D89)
1. **One read.** `GET /api/reports/sales?from&to&group_by&branch_id&employee_id&category_id&product_id&sale_type&sort&page&page_size` → `public.report_sales(p_from date, p_to date, p_group_by text, p_branch_id uuid DEFAULT NULL, p_employee_id uuid DEFAULT NULL, p_category_id uuid DEFAULT NULL, p_product_id uuid DEFAULT NULL, p_sale_type text DEFAULT NULL, p_sort text DEFAULT 'revenue_desc', p_page integer DEFAULT 1, p_page_size integer DEFAULT 50)`. It answers `{ from, to, group_by, filters, generated_at, can_see_cost, rows: [...], totals: {...}, check: {...}, total_count, page, page_size }`. Every number is a SQL aggregate (`numeric`, rounded once at the output, sent as a string); Node only parses and forwards.
2. **The period and the filters.** `p_from` ≤ `p_to`, at most **366 days** (`VALIDATION_FAILED: period`), dates are **business dates** (the shop's day, as K04 and S07), both inclusive. `p_branch_id` NULL = every branch the caller can see (`app.has_branch_access`; inactive branches included: old sales stay reportable); a given branch must be the shop's (`BRANCH_NOT_FOUND`) and visible (`BRANCH_DENIED`). `p_employee_id` = the seller (`sales.created_by`; must be the shop's: `EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`). `p_category_id` = that category **and all its sub-categories** (a recursive walk of `parent_id`; `CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`). `p_product_id` (`PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`). `p_sale_type` `cash` | `installment` (decision 6). Every id of another shop answers its `*_NOT_FOUND`, never data.
3. **Which documents count.**
   - **Sales:** every sale whose `business_date` is in the period, in the filtered branches, **except voided sales** (a sale with a `sales_returns` row of kind `void`): a void is a same-day full reversal (S07), so the sale and its void both stay out, exactly D01's "sales not voided". An exchange's new sale (`sales_returns.exchange_sale_id`) is an ordinary sale.
   - **Returns:** every `sales_returns` row of kind `return` or `exchange` whose own `business_date` is in the period (not the sale's date), in the filtered branches, whatever the date of the sale it returns (a return in October of a September sale lowers October). Kind `void` never counts (above).
   - A sale that is partly or fully returned stays in the sales columns at its full amount; its returns appear in the returns columns of the period in which they happened.
4. **The measures** (per row and in `totals`; money 2 decimals, `margin_pct` 1 decimal or `null` when revenue is 0):
   - `sales_count` = distinct sales in the row; `quantity` = Σ `sale_lines.quantity`.
   - `revenue` = Σ (`line_total` − `tax_amount`): **the amount after discounts, without VAT** (VAT is the state's money, not revenue). `gross` = Σ `line_total` (what the customer paid, VAT included). `tax` = Σ `tax_amount`. `discount` = Σ `discount_amount` (the S03 discounts already inside `line_total`; shown for information).
   - `cost` = Σ (`quantity` × `unit_cost`) at 4 decimals, rounded once per row and once for `totals` (the cost snapshot taken at the sale, D02 of the data model; never today's average cost).
   - `margin` = `revenue` − `cost`; `margin_pct` = `margin` / `revenue` × 100.
   - Returns, same definitions on `sales_return_lines` of the counted returns: `return_count` (distinct returns), `returned_quantity`, `returned_revenue` = Σ (`refund_amount` − `tax_amount`), `returned_tax`, `returned_cost` = Σ (`quantity` × `unit_cost`), `returned_margin` = `returned_revenue` − `returned_cost`.
   - Net: `net_quantity` = `quantity` − `returned_quantity`, `net_revenue`, `net_cost`, `net_margin`, `net_margin_pct` (same rules).
   - **Installment profit is not margin.** For installment sales the sale lines carry the **cash price** (N02); the contract's profit (`contracts.markup_amount`) is the price of deferral, fixed at signing (rule 1) and shown **apart**: `contract_profit` = Σ `markup_amount` of the contracts of the row's sales (by sale; in a product / category grouping it is split over the sale's lines in proportion to their `line_total`, the last line of a sale taking the rest, and a note on the page says so), and `contract_profit_returned` = the profit shares S05 removed for the counted returns (the `contract_changes` rows of kind `return_adjustment` linked to them: read D88 decision 8 for the share). It is never added to `margin` (owner question 1).
5. **Who sees what.** The report needs `report.view`. Reads work in an **expired** shop (P05), so don't use `app.require_permission` (it refuses an expired shop): do as D01's `get_owner_dashboard` does (`app.current_employee_id()` for a signed-in employee of the shop, then `app.has_permission('report.view')`, else `PERMISSION_DENIED`; check the code name in `errors.ts`). Cost, margin, contract profit and every derived column need **`report.financial`** as well; without it they are `null`, `can_see_cost` is `false`, sorting by them is refused (`VALIDATION_FAILED: sort`), and the Web hides those columns. Branch scope as decision 2. Not audited (reads aren't, D19).
6. **Group by** (`p_group_by`, required, one of):
   | `group_by` | Row key and label | Notes |
   |---|---|---|
   | `day` | `business_date` | every day of the period that has a sale or a return; days with neither are left out |
   | `month` | first day of the month, label `YYYY-MM` | |
   | `branch` | branch id, name, code | returns go to the return's branch (S05: always the sale's) |
   | `employee` | employee id, name | sales by the seller; **returns by the seller of the returned sale** (not by who took the return back), so the net is the seller's (owner question 3) |
   | `category` | category id and name of the product's **current** category, `null` = "No category" | top-level roll-up: `category_level=top` query flag groups by the root of each category's tree (default `leaf`) |
   | `product` | product id, name, SKU | |
   | `sale_type` | `cash` / `installment` | `installment` = the sale has a contract (`contracts.sale_id`, any status); `cash` = everything else |
   | `payment_type` | `cash`, `bank_transfer`, `store_credit`, `on_contract` | decision 7 |
   A return line takes the dimensions of **its sale line** (product, category, branch, seller, sale type), and of the return's date for `day` / `month`. The filters of decision 2 apply to sales and returns the same way (a product filter keeps only that product's sale lines and return lines).
7. **Payment type is money received, not goods.** A sale's lines can't be split by how the customer paid without inventing an allocation, so `group_by=payment_type` answers money only: per method `amount` = what the counted sales took by that method (K04's `paid_by_method` definition, D84 decision 2: the payment allocations to the sale, the down payment of an installment sale included) and `on_contract` = the part financed by a contract (`total` − down payment); refunds of the counted returns by `refund_method` (cash, bank transfer, store credit, contract adjustment = `on_contract`) in the returns columns; `net`. Quantity, cost and margin are `null` in this grouping and the page says why. Σ of the methods + `on_contract` = Σ `sales.total` of the counted sales (test it).
8. **Totals and the self-check (the slice's Done when).** `totals` is computed **from the document headers**, not from the rows: `sales_count`, `gross` = Σ `sales.total`, `tax` = Σ `sales.tax_total`, `revenue` = `gross` − `tax`, `discount` = Σ `sales.discount_total`, `cost` = Σ `sales.cost_total`; returns: Σ `sales_returns.total`, `tax_total`, cost from their lines. `check` answers `{ rows_match: boolean, differences: { revenue, tax, cost, returned_revenue } }`: the sum of the rows of **all pages** (computed in SQL) minus `totals`. Revenue and tax must match exactly (every `line_total` and `tax_amount` is already 2 decimals and the header is the sum of its lines: if not, that is a bug to report, not to hide); cost may differ by the per-sale rounding of `cost_total` (at most 0.01 per counted sale): `rows_match` allows exactly that and D89 says so. A row filter (employee, category, product, sale type) makes the headers no longer comparable: then `totals` is the sum of the rows and `check` is `null`.
9. **Sorting and paging.** `p_sort` one of `revenue_desc` (default), `revenue_asc`, `margin_desc`, `margin_asc`, `quantity_desc`, `net_revenue_desc`, `key_asc` (the row key: date, name). `day` and `month` default to `key_asc`. Ties by the key. `p_page_size` 1-1000 (the Web's export uses 1000), `total_count` = rows of the whole result. `totals` and `check` always cover the whole result, not the page.
10. **Speed.** Under **500 ms** for one month of the seed shop and under **2 s** for a year of a shop with 50 000 sales / 150 000 lines (measure on the local stack with a generated set, as D01 did, and write the numbers in D89). Use the existing indexes (`sales_branch_date_idx`, `sales_returns_date_idx`, `sale_lines` by sale); add one only if a measured plan needs it and say which. No summary table, no cache.

## Owner questions (recommended answer after the arrow; build on it unless the owner answers otherwise, and record the answers in D89)
1. **Installment profit:** shown in its own column ("contract profit"), never added to the margin, which is cash price minus cost for every sale, *or* added to the margin of installment sales. → **Own column.**
2. **Revenue:** after discounts and **without VAT** (gross with VAT shown as its own column), *or* with VAT. → **Without VAT.**
3. **Returns by employee:** counted against the seller of the returned sale (net sales per seller), *or* against the employee who took the return back. → **The seller.**
4. **Voided sales:** left out entirely (the sale and its void), as on the D01 dashboard, *or* shown as a sale plus a return. → **Left out.**
5. **Category of an old sale:** the product's current category (simple; a product moved to another category moves its history), *or* the category at the time of the sale (needs a snapshot column on `sale_lines`: a schema change). → **Current category.**
