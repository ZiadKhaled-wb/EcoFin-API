## Goal
One page shows everything about a customer's money: what they bought, what they returned, their installment contracts with the schedules, what they paid, what is overdue, what they still owe, their documents and their guarantors. A user can print a statement of the account for a date range.

## Slice text (R02)
- **Includes:** purchases, returns, contracts, schedules, payments, overdue amounts, open balance, documents, guarantors; printable statement for a date range.
- **Done when:** the open balance equals contract totals − payments − returns.

- *Default, to confirm with the owner:* build now rather than wait for N02/N03. If the owner says wait, this file still holds; only the start date moves.

## Decisions (record them in D65)
1. **Read only.** R02 adds no table, column, enum, permission key or write function. It adds two `STABLE` SQL functions and two routes. No audit row: these are reads.
2. **Who may read an account:** the caller must pass `app.can_read_customers` (R01's read keys; also works in an expired shop) and the shop's plan must have the `customers` module (`MODULE_NOT_IN_PLAN` otherwise). Another shop's customer, or an unknown id, gives `CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND` (404). *Default:* no extra key. A cashier taking an installment payment needs to see the balance.
3. **Shop-wide.** The account covers every branch of the shop, like the supplier statement (D53). Each row carries its `branch_id`; there is no branch filter in V1.
4. **Money words (one meaning everywhere: API, screen, statement):**
   - `contracts_total` = Σ `contracts.total_payable` of the customer's contracts, **every status**. `total_payable` = financed + markup, so the down payment is not in it. The down payment is paid at the till with the sale.
   - `paid` = Σ effective allocations (`v_effective_allocations`, so reversed payments don't count) to the `schedule_lines` of those contracts, **every schedule version**. Fee payments are not in it.
   - `returned` = Σ `contract_changes.amount` with kind `return_adjustment` on those contracts.
   - `written_off` = Σ `contract_changes.amount` with kind `write_off`. `settlement_discount` = Σ `contract_changes.amount` with kind `early_settlement`. `reschedule_change` = Σ `contract_changes.amount` with kind `reschedule` (signed: + when rescheduling adds profit).
   - **`open_balance` = `contracts_total` − `paid` − `returned` − `written_off` − `settlement_discount` + `reschedule_change`.** The slice's Done when is the case where the last three are 0. They stay 0 until N05 exists.
   - `fees_due` (late fees charged − waived − paid, from `v_contract_balances`) is shown **beside** the open balance and is **not part of it**. It counts as money owed, but it has its own line, so the slice's formula stays true.
   - `overdue_amount`, `overdue_lines`, `max_days_late`, `next_due_date` come from `v_contract_balances` over contracts in status `active` / `rescheduled`. `aging` (not yet due, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+) comes from `v_receivables_aging`.
   - `store_credit` = the customer's `store_credit_balances.balance`. This is money the shop owes the customer, so it is shown separately and never netted against the open balance.
5. **A rule for later slices (N02, N03, N05, S05 must carry it in their specs):** whatever lowers or raises what a customer owes on a contract is either a payment allocated to a schedule line, or one `contract_changes` row whose `amount` has the meaning in decision 4. With that rule the open balance stays equal to Σ `v_contract_balances.remaining` of the customer's contracts. R02's test checks both numbers on every scenario it seeds, so a later slice that breaks the rule breaks R02's test.
6. **Cash purchases are listed, not owed.** A sale paid in full at the till (S01/S02) has `v_sale_balances.unpaid` = 0 and adds nothing to the open balance. An installment sale's financed part sits on its contract. If any sale of the customer has `unpaid` ≠ 0 (only possible from a bug), the account answers it in `issues` (decision 12) instead of hiding it in the balance.
7. **Sections of the account** (`GET /api/customers/:id/account`), every list newest first, at most 200 rows per list, with `*_count` totals so the screen can say "showing 200 of N":
   - `customer`: R01's `Customer` (with R01's masking).
   - `summary`: the money words of decision 4, plus `credit_limit`, `exposure` and `available_credit` from `v_customer_exposure` (R03 will use them; shown read-only here).
   - `purchases`: the customer's sales: number, date, branch, kind, total, paid at the till, financed (the contract's `financed_amount`, or 0), returned, `contract_id` (or null).
   - `returns`: the customer's `sales_returns` (through the sale): number, date, sale number, kind, refund method, total.
   - `contracts`: one row per contract from `v_contract_balances`: number, date, branch, status, cash price, down payment, total payable, paid, remaining, overdue amount, fees due, next due date, installments count, frequency, and its `schedule`: the current version's lines from `v_schedule_line_status` (seq, due date, amount, paid, remaining, status, days late). Superseded lines are not listed; `schedule_version` says which version is shown.
   - `payments`: the customer's incoming payments (`payments.customer_id`, direction `in`), every method: number, date, branch, method, amount, reference, what it paid (sale number, contract number and seq, or "fee"), and `reversed` (true when a reversal exists). Reversal rows themselves are listed with `reverses_number`.
   - `documents`: `files` rows for the customer and for their contracts (entity `customer` or `contract`). Purpose `customer_id` (ID photos) is listed only with `customer.view_pii`; without it the list leaves them out and `id_photos_count` still says how many there are (R01's rule). No file bytes here: the screen opens a file through F14's signed URL route.
   - `guarantors`: from `customer_guarantors` and `contract_guarantors`: name, phone, relation, the contract numbers they guarantee, `national_id_masked`, and `national_id` only with `customer.view_pii` (R01's `app.mask_national_id`; reuse it, don't write a second one).
8. **Statement** (`GET /api/customers/:id/statement?from=&to=`), shop-wide, the supplier statement's shape (D53). Rows in date order, then by creation time:

   | kind | debit (raises what they owe) | credit (lowers it) |
   |---|---|---|
   | `contract` (issued) | `total_payable` | 0 |
   | `installment_payment` (allocation to a schedule line) | 0 | allocated amount |
   | `payment_reversal` | the reversed allocation | 0 |
   | `return_adjustment` | 0 | `amount` |
   | `write_off`, `early_settlement` | 0 | `amount` |
   | `reschedule` | `amount` if > 0 | `−amount` if < 0 |
   | `sale` (paid at the till) | 0 | 0 (the row shows `amount` = sale total, for information) |
   | `sale_return` (refunded in cash or store credit) | 0 | 0 (`amount` = return total) |

   - `opening_balance` = the net of every row before `from` (0 without `from`). `balance` on each row is the running total after it. `closing_balance` = opening + Σ debit − Σ credit of the rows shown. **Without dates, `closing_balance` = `summary.open_balance`.**
   - `from`, `to` are business dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`), both optional; `from > to` → `VALIDATION_FAILED`. *Default:* no maximum range.
   - A payment that pays a fee is **not** on the statement in V1 (fees are not in the open balance). The screen says so in a note under the table.
   - The statement also answers `shop` (name, logo file id, tax registration no.) and `printed_at` (server time), so the print layout needs no other call.
9. **Dates.** A row's date is its `business_date` where the table has one (`payments`, `sales`), otherwise the shop-time date of `created_at`. Contracts use `created_at`'s shop-time date, not `first_due_date`.
10. **PII in the statement.** The printed statement shows `national_id_masked` only, **even for a caller with `customer.view_pii`**. A printed page leaves the system. *Default*, to confirm with the owner.
11. **Performance.** One SQL function per route, returning `jsonb`. Index use: every list filters by `organization_id` and `customer_id` (or `contract_id IN (the customer's contracts)`). If a needed index is missing from the baseline, add it in this migration. That is the only DDL allowed, and you name it in D65.
12. **`issues`** (list, usually empty) on the account: `sale_unpaid` (decision 6) and `balance_mismatch` (open balance ≠ Σ `v_contract_balances.remaining`, decision 5). Each issue has a code and the document number. The screen shows them in a warning box for users with `report.financial`, and hides them from everyone else.

## Database facts (baseline; R02 changes nothing)
- `contracts`: `number, customer_id, branch_id, sale_id | order_id, cash_price, down_payment, financed_amount, markup_amount, total_payable (= financed + markup, CHECK), installments_count, frequency, first_due_date, status contract_status (active, completed, rescheduled, written_off, returned)`.
- `schedule_lines`: `contract_id, version, seq, due_date, principal, profit, amount, superseded`. Paid and overdue status come from `v_schedule_line_status` (`paid, remaining, fees_due, days_late, status`: superseded / paid / closed / overdue / partly_paid / due / upcoming).
- `contract_changes`: `contract_id, kind change_kind (early_settlement, reschedule, write_off, return_adjustment), old_version, new_version, amount, reason, approved_by`.
- `payments` / `payment_allocations`: an allocation has exactly one of `sale_id / order_id / schedule_line_id / charge_id / return_id`. `v_effective_allocations` drops reversed payments and the reversals themselves.
- `sales_returns`: `number, sale_id, branch_id, kind (return, void, exchange), refund_method (cash, bank_transfer, store_credit, contract_adjustment), total`.
- `files`: purposes include `customer_id`, `contract_pdf`, `promissory_note`, `signature`, `legal_document`.
- `v_customer_exposure`: `credit_limit, exposure, available_credit, overdue_amount, fees_due, open_contracts, store_credit`.

## Part B: client (EcoFin Web)
1. Contracts 0.34.0.
2. `customersApi.account(id)` and `customersApi.statement(id, { from, to })`.
3. **Customer page** `/customers/:id` gets tabs: **Details** (R01's page as it is), **Account**, **Statement**.
   - **Account tab:**
     - summary cards at the top: Open balance (large), Overdue (red when > 0, with the max days late), Fees due, Paid, Store credit. If the shop has the installments module: Credit limit / Available.
     - then sections: Contracts (each row expands to its schedule with a status badge per line), Payments, Purchases, Returns, Guarantors, Documents.
     - every section has an empty state ("No contracts yet").
     - numbers link to their documents where a page exists (`/sales/:id`); otherwise plain text.
   - **Statement tab:** `from` / `to` date pickers (default: the first day of the current month to today), the table (date, kind, number, contract, debit, credit, balance), opening and closing rows, and a note that fees are not included. A **Print** button calls `window.print()`.
   - **Print layout** (`@media print`): shop name, logo and tax no.; customer name, phones and **masked** ID; the date range; the table; totals; printed-at time; page numbers if the browser supports them. The menu, tabs, filters and buttons are `print:hidden`. RTL in Arabic, LTR in English. Money is formatted with the shop's number helper; never floats.
4. **i18n:** `customers` namespace (`account.*`, `statement.*`), ar + en, same keys. No hard-coded text, including kind and status labels.
5. **Tests:**
   - the Account tab in ar **and** en with a fixture that has a contract, a schedule, two payments (one reversed), a return adjustment and a guarantor;
   - the open balance shown = the fixture's `summary.open_balance`;
   - the overdue card is red only when overdue > 0;
   - empty states;
   - ID photos are listed only when the fixture user has `customer.view_pii`;
   - the statement sends `from` / `to`, shows the opening and closing rows, and the print button calls `window.print`;
   - the statement never shows the full national ID;
   - the issues box is shown only with `report.financial`.
