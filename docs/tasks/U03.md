## Goal
An accountant or owner pays a supplier from a vault or bank account. The payment covers one or several of the supplier's approved invoices, fully or partly, and can't exceed what's owed. The money leaves the account through the cash ledger. The payment gets a number, and a **double-click pays once**. The owner can reverse a payment made by mistake, with a reason.

## Slice text (U03)
- **Includes:** pay one or several invoices, full or partial, from a vault (cash ledger out); can't pay more than owed; payment receipt numbered; idempotent.
- **Done when:** a double-click pays once; invoice and supplier balances update.

## Decisions already made (record them in D58; the ones marked *default* are to confirm with the owner)
1. **Every payment is fully allocated.**
   - The allocations add up to the amount, each one ≤ that invoice's current `outstanding` (`v_purchase_invoice_balances`).
   - Without allocations in the request, the server allocates **oldest invoice first** (`invoice_date`, then `number`).
   - The amount can't exceed the supplier's total outstanding → `PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`, details `{ purchase_invoice_id|null, outstanding }`.
   - No payments "on account".
2. **The invoices:**
   - approved, of **this** supplier, outstanding > 0, each at most once (`ALLOCATION_INVALID`);
   - in branches the caller can access.
3. **The account:**
   - an **active** `safe` or `bank` (`CASH_ACCOUNT_INACTIVE` / `VAULT_INVALID`);
   - of the payment's branch, or an org-wide bank (`branch_id NULL`);
   - the payment's `branch_id` is chosen by the user, who needs access to it.
   - The cash ledger trigger refuses a balance below 0 (`INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`), unless the account allows negatives (banks with `allow_negative_bank`).
4. **Ledger row:** `cash_movements` amount **−amount**, type `supplier_payment`, source `supplier_payment`, `business_date` = today (shop date). The `DAY_CLOSED` rules apply.
5. **Idempotent:** the same key → the first payment, no second cash movement. A `system:` key is refused.
6. **Reversal** (*default: owner only*):
   - `reverse_supplier_payment` creates a linked `supplier_payments` row (`reverses_payment_id`, the same amount, `reason` 3–500) and a `cash_movements` row **+amount** with `reverses_movement_id` = the original movement;
   - a payment can be reversed once (`PAYMENT_ALREADY_REVERSED`; the baseline unique index is the last line);
   - a reversal can't be reversed;
   - the baseline views then exclude both, so the balances go back up;
   - the caller must be the **OWNER** (`APPROVAL_OWNER_ONLY`, reused from U06) and hold `supplier.payment_create`.
7. **Due dates are display only:** `invoice_date + supplier.payment_terms_days`, with an overdue flag. No interest, no blocking.

## Part A: server + contracts
1. **Migration** `20261011140000_u03_supplier_payments.sql`. No new tables.
2. `public.post_supplier_payment(p_supplier_id, p_branch_id, p_cash_account_id, p_amount, p_reference, p_allocations jsonb, p_idempotency_key)`, `supplier.payment_create`:
   1. Lock the key; the same key → the first payment.
   2. Check the supplier (the shop's), the branch access and the account (decision 3).
   3. **Lock** the supplier's approved invoices involved (`FOR UPDATE`, ordered by id), so two payments can't both use the same outstanding.
   4. Allocations: validate them (decisions 1–2), or build them oldest first.
   5. Number from `next_document_number(org, branch, 'supplier_payment')`.
   6. Insert the payment, the allocations and the cash movement (decision 4).
   7. One audit row `supplier_payment.posted` (`{number, amount, allocations, account}`).
